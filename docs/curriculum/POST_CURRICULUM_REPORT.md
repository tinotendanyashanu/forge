This phase produced documentation, read-only aggregate queries and disposable local workflow checks. It did not implement product features, change mastery rules, award human verification or add a content batch.

## What the baseline actually contains

The post-release read-only snapshot at **11:06:44 UTC** contains three learner records, three assessment sessions, three served items and three graded answers. All three first submitted choice answers were incorrect. There are three mastery-evidence rows, three skill snapshots in `insufficient_evidence`, three review cards and three review events. All three review events are the initial event for their learner/skill; there are **no later due reviews**, no repeat assessment exposures, no repeated learner/subskill error sequence, and **zero recorded lesson opens or completions**.

These are database records, not evidence of three independent human participants. Anonymous identities and unknown activity provenance mean they could include smoke tests or multiple identities for one person. The three errors cannot establish item difficulty, a lesson defect, retention, transfer or a population learning gain. There is no post-lesson or delayed-recall outcome to report. Do not rank lessons or describe the three initial review events as successful/failed retention trials.

Evidence: [aggregate baseline](validation/post-curriculum-baseline.json), [exact queries and query digest](validation/post-curriculum-queries.json). Queries ran in a consistent read-only transaction and returned aggregates, not learner text or identities. Several learner-facing GET endpoints call mastery synchronization, so those endpoints were deliberately not used as a supposedly read-only analytics export. See [`_state` and mastery routes](../../api/app/api/routes/mastery.py) and [`sync_learner`](../../api/app/mastery/service.py).

## Existing capabilities and missing capabilities

| Measure | What exists now | What remains missing or limited |

| First-attempt correctness | `Answer` links learner, question and session item; `submitted_at` plus the unique item answer and deterministic `EvaluationResult` allow the first submitted choice answer per learner/question to be derived. Duplicate submissions return 409. | No first-attempt report/filter in the current UI. First submitted is different from first exposure or unaided recall. Assessment hints are visible but their use is not recorded. |
| Repeated errors by concept | Join graded answers to `Question.topic_id` for repeated errors per learner/subskill, distinguishing repeats of one question from misses on several questions. The gaps engine already reports repeated failures and AI-derived misconception/missing-concept signals. | Subskill is a coarse concept proxy. Objective wrong choices are not mapped to a normalized, validated misconception event. AI signals remain unverified; an error count does not diagnose the learner's reasoning. |
| Performance after reading | `LessonRead.first_opened_at` and `completed_at`, lesson/check mappings and `SessionItem.served_at` can identify attempts served after recorded completion of a matching subskill lesson. The audit query uses an `EXISTS` join to avoid counting an answer multiple times. | The actual lesson page does not call the existing `/open` endpoint. `Mark as read` creates both timestamps on first use. No dwell time, reread history, completion version, launch-to-lesson attribution or causal comparison exists. Completion is an honest self-report, not proof of reading. |
| Exposure and repeats | Each served session item has `served_at`; another session creates another item. Refreshing an unanswered item keeps its UUID. Answers and interviews also identify their questions. | Serving is not proof of display or attention. There is no consolidated cross-mode exposure report. Unanswered prior serves do not make an item "answered" for selection. No durable objective hint-use or session-purpose event is stored. |
| Delayed recall / spaced review | Skill-level SM-2 cards and per-evidence review events store due dates, interval before/after, quality, `was_due` and timestamps. Early success does not extend the interval; failure reschedules for tomorrow. The review UI launches an assessment/interview. | The first evidence is immediately a review event. Any eligible evidence can update a skill card; an event does not prove a dedicated review-button visit or recall of that same question/concept. The due rule has a 12-hour tolerance. Exclude initial events and inspect elapsed time, source, question identity and assistance before describing delayed recall. |
| Unfamiliar-question performance | The selector prefers questions not previously **answered** by that learner. SQL can identify a first recorded assessment serve, including earlier unanswered serves when establishing novelty. | There is no held-out pool, assessment-purpose/origin flag, reservation mechanism or validated transfer protocol. An unseen-in-this-assessment item may have appeared in an interview, lesson, another identity or external study. First bank exposure is not demonstrated transfer to an unfamiliar engineering problem. |
| Completion versus mastery | Lesson completion writes `lesson_reads` only. It does not add mastery evidence. Objective grading, pending free-text assessment answers and advisory AI interview evidence are separated. Existing tests and the new ten-launch probe confirm the separation. | No joined completion/outcome report exists. A skill badge does not establish every subskill, unaided performance or unfamiliar-task success. ## Assessment validity and content quality

There are **135 scored choice questions and 23 unscored free-text assessment questions**. Skill-level coverage has three scored questions per difficulty, but the finer 45-subskill × 3-difficulty matrix has **27 cells with no scored question and 110 cells with fewer than two**. Dedicated lesson coverage of 45/45 therefore does not establish full subskill/difficulty assessment coverage or independent transfer evidence. The current launch button filters skill/difficulty, so its session can contain other subskills than the lesson just read.

The [content inventory](validation/post-curriculum-content-inventory.json) records every cell. It also records **118 question metadata entries, all `reviewed`, 40 questions without those metadata entries, and 45 lessons, all `reviewed`**. No human verification was awarded. The existing gate's 30 warnings are screening signals; a near-duplicate or length warning needs human inspection, not automatic rewriting. Authored answer-key imbalance is already mitigated by per-item option permutation; preserve that behavior and use stable option IDs in analysis.

Question/lesson records are not immutable historical assessment versions. A current question's wording, taxonomy or options can otherwise be joined to older answers. Freeze the release commit and reviewed artifact hash in each evaluation record outside the app. Any approved future meaning/key change to an answered question needs a new stable slug and controlled release; preserve prior records. Do not mix differently worded revisions under one item statistic.

## Prioritized improvement plan

**P0 — establish trustworthy observation and human review now, without product changes.** Use the existing records plus the supplied read-only queries. Define the unit as learner record × question × attempt and report raw counts, unique learner records, distinct items, prior exposure and elapsed delay. Keep synthetic tests separate. Do not call current first-attempt data "unaided" or completion data "reading time." Start the [human checklist](HUMAN_REVIEW.md), one signed artifact at a time. Prioritize a prerequisite lesson and its checks for the learner's chosen study target, plus the technically consequential shell/container/incident examples. Investigate actual repeated errors before expanding scope.

Acceptance: an unsigned checklist remains unsigned; every accepted item has a human-authored verdict tied to a final hash; no incorrect source claim or material ambiguity is left unresolved; the first monthly report labels empty post-lesson/retention cohorts as unavailable. Start provenance work on the 40 legacy questions when they are actually reviewed, without inventing prior review.

**P1 — propose small measurement/workflow changes for separate approval.** The highest-value candidates are wiring actual lesson opens to the existing endpoint, durably recording objective hint use, retaining lesson/session purpose attribution, and presenting first-versus-repeat counts from existing records. Opening telemetry can reuse the current model but still cannot prove attention; repeats and revision history need a separate design if required. Objective hint use needs a durable source record and ingestion change, not a backfill of guessed zeroes. A first-pass report can remain a read-only query instead of an admin system. Review acceptance should be based on exercised events and accurate labels, with no silent mastery-rule change.

**P1 — review scoring and feedback on the selected concepts.** Have a human solve items blind, inspect distractor plausibility and feedback, and check whether the exercise discriminates copying from explanation/application. Compare a repeat of a known item with a different task using the same concept. Use patterns in actual attempts and the coverage matrix to choose a small correction proposal. Do not automatically replace every flagged question or fill all 27 empty cells as another batch.

**P2 — improve independent assessment only after the pilot identifies the need.** Consider a small held-out or equivalent-task protocol and explicit practice/evaluation origin, plus immutable revision attribution if the existing Git-based study record proves insufficient. These require separate design/approval. Item discrimination, calibrated mastery thresholds, randomized lesson-effect estimates and adaptive scoring are not credible deliverables from this baseline. No such feature or rule change is implemented here.

## Study and evaluation plan: November 10–December 9

Treat this as a formative individual pilot, not a trial proving FORGE's efficacy. Budget three 25–35 minute study sessions a week, plus brief due reviews that fit the existing queue. Choose **two target subskills** and their prerequisite lessons from the current curriculum; depth is preferable to attempting all 45 in a month. A human reviewer owns the review/sign-off log; the learner owns honest assistance/exposure notes; a maintainer runs aggregate queries and protects release boundaries. Names remain unassigned until those people accept the roles.

1. **November 10–16: baseline and correctness.** Record the release/hash and target subskills. Before opening the target lesson, take the relevant existing assessment closed-book where feasible and record which actual served questions match the target. Log prior familiarity and any hint/external help manually. Complete the prerequisite and one target lesson, solve its practice exercise, then mark it read honestly. A human reviews the target lesson and linked checks using the checklist. Do not chase a mastery badge by repeating the same answers.
2. **November 17–23: delayed retrieval and feedback.** Follow the existing due queue and do an agreed check around one week after study, recording actual elapsed time. Attempt before reopening explanations, then inspect feedback and write a short explanation of the error or trade-off. Repeat with the second target subskill. Keep known-item retrieval separate from first recorded item attempts and unscored written reasoning. Record assistance outside the app until instrumentation exists.
3. **November 24–30: transfer and instructional diagnosis.** A human selects one small unfamiliar application per target concept, drawing on the current lesson's transfer objective but withholding the concrete task until evaluation. Use a local disposable environment or a verbal/written design problem; keep the production sandbox disabled. Fix the rubric before seeing the response and record criterion-level evidence. This is manual evaluation outside the scored bank, not an automatic mastery credit or a new large content batch. Review any concept missed twice, checking prerequisite gaps and the explanation before proposing a revision.
4. **December 1–7: retention check.** Revisit both concepts after a further gap, using the due queue and a planned check around day 21/28 where practical. Record the actual delay; due status alone is insufficient. Separate same-item recall, another bank item, and the unfamiliar application. If there is no qualifying delayed trial, report no delayed-recall result. Keep a prerequisite/readiness issue separate from a defective question.
5. **December 8–9: evaluate and choose the next small change.** Summarize first submissions, repeats, matched post-completion attempts, delayed objective attempts and manual transfer criteria with their denominators. Report empty cohorts, item exposure, hint uncertainty, missing sessions and anonymous-identity limitations. Include exercise artifacts and human sign-offs. Recommend only the smallest evidenced correction or measurement change, with its proposed validation and separate approval. Do not claim causal improvement from before/after percentages or this small self-selected pilot.

The [study log template](STUDY_LOG_TEMPLATE.md) captures the gaps without changing the app. The one-week and later checkpoints are practical planning windows, not claims of an optimal spacing schedule. Retrieval practice and distributing study across time have supporting research; that supports trying the protocol, not asserting that FORGE has already produced those benefits. See [Roediger and Karpicke's primary experiment](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/) and [Dunlosky et al.'s review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266).

At month end, success means a trustworthy, small set of observed attempts and reviewed artifacts that can guide revisions. It does not require a claimed learning gain, a target lesson-count increase or a newly awarded mastery status.
